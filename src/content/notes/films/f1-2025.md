The reason you watch 24 Formula 1 races in a year is that there’s something visceral about watching a tightly engineered and highly-refined machine on the screen. You just can’t look away. That’s the feeling I got from this movie. 

What bugs me about F1 (as a longtime fan) is that all its media properties—shows, games, social media, and now this movie—paint a picture of a sport filled with earnest and well-meaning people differentiated only by the colors on their cars. In the F1 media universe they tell stories of plucky teams that win it all through of their dedication to hard work and to following their neatly-storyboarded character arcs. In the F1 movie, a pair of corporations (and no doubt several sovereign wealth funds) together spend vast sums of money to weave a story that the same money renders as a pipe dream in real life. 

In pursuit of this ideal the producers chose—rather than creating a novel setting—to render the real 2024 F1 season as a series of winks to the connected content consumer. In this world drivers, cars, engineers, fans, and the real life outcomes from 2024 take a backseat to selling the idea that any sufficiently motivated Guy could win an F1 race. In particular the footage of drivers becomes so jarringly uncanny that you can only really laugh. 

Still, though, I loved this movie for the same reason I watch the races and play the games. The lights go out, the cars roar to life, and the storylines go away just to watch what happens on the track. I was glued to my seat for the whole film just to catch every second of the racing. The camera work, sound, and music all come together to create such a thrilling experience that you can’t help but walk out of the theater feeling like you got everything you came to see. 

As a total package it’s a bit cringey and polished to a sheen and plays very safe. But it accomplishes all its goals and I think succeeds in spite of itself. Great summer movie.
