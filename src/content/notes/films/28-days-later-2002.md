All zombie/apocalypse media eventually overstays its welcome for me so I'm surprised that this was as good as I remembered.

28 DAYS LATER is undeniably an archetypal example of 2000s visual aesthetics. Its 480p-ish digital photography only serves to underline this point. Its visual style is defined by grungy, muddled backgrounds punctuated by brightly-colored principal subjects, emphasized by dynamic camera movements, repetitive emphasizing shots, and moody lighting. Several individual shots in the film, particularly in act 3, could be lifted directly from 2000s-era pop punk music videos.

Most interesting apocalypse media holds a funhouse mirror to the conventions of the communities where they're set. Most of my zombie media diet, like THE WALKING DEAD, THE LAST OF US, and LEFT FOR DEAD, is comfortably situated in the American south and west. When the norms of society go away, the independence and solitude of America's rural communities give way to isolation, distrust, and paranoia. England, by contrast, is defined by lots of traditions, social hierarchies, and distinct geographic differences on a small island. So the funhouse mirror of the Infection lays bare that--in the eyes of this film's creators--without rigid social constraints, people become really angry apes. Gross!

Lots of zombie media reaches for the heights of this one but it made me just as uncomfortable now as it did when I first watched it a decade ago. Planning to watch the sequels in short order.
