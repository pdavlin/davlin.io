Would never sit down and watch this unless I was watching it on my indoor bike trainer. I don't think it's inherently bad. Sebastian Stan is passable as Trump, and it's not a pastiche performance of Trump. Jeremy Strong is good, but I think "just do Kendal Roy, but old" is really safe for him.

My problem with this movie is that it has nothing interesting to say, probably because Donald Trump is too important, too recent, and too dishonest to build a coherent, believable narrative of his life. This movie has almost a pathological lack of anything to say about Trump. It believes he's Roy Cohn 2.0, that he's selfish and cruel, but it goes out of its way to avoid *showing* Trump's mistreatment of tenants and workers, and wants us to buy all this because a) we know who Trump is because we are constantly reading about him, and b) other characters are constantly offering these critiques of him directly. But I think that failing to show his role in the awful things he does continues his lifelong narrative that he's uninvolved.

I know beyond a shadow of a doubt that I'm going to have to sit through at least twenty more "serious" biographical films about Donald Trump in my lifetime. Hopefully, eventually, one of them will have something to say about him.
