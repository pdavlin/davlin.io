A visual and auditory masterpiece in IMAX. Villenueve's direction is perfect to tell this story and as a technical accomplishment it stands alone among science fiction movies. It has such a sense of scale, but what's happening on screen always feels clear. Unbelievable thought and care is put into the visual language of families, tribes, Houses, and planets. This is the kind of directing Zack Snyder aspires to--you could pull almost any still image in this film and frame it, but it still works as a narrative.

I feel that telling this story in two parts perfectly highlights the cruelty of the Houses and the Empire, and I'm glad the film doesn't shy away from that narrative. In the first movie, the Fremen are fearsome and deadly, and the fact that they can navigate the desert is the subject of awe. But in Part 2, highlighted by Javier Bardem's Stilgar, the Fremen are depicted as naïve, open to belief and swayed by the actions of someone they view as a savior. Chalamet's performance throughout is perfect--it kept me wondering whether his shift reflects that Paul always had an underlying goal to exploit the Fremen, or whether drinking the Water of Life changed that in him. Zendaya and Rebecca Ferguson do a great job as the metaphorical angel and devil on Paul's shoulders.

There are some weaknesses; Brolin's work as Halleck is wasted here just to keep Chani out of key story moments. The last third of the movie moves nearly at breakneck speed, and the plot resolves too quickly. I would've liked to experience more tension between the Emperor and Paul before its resolution. The scenes on Giedi Prime are a little too comfortably Nazi for my taste, and I think in light of the cramped third act I wonder whether they could've been cut down.

I loved this movie. I'll probably try and watch it again in IMAX before it leaves theaters. What a spectacle. I hope that Villenueve gets to make Messiah, but I'm not sure I trust Warner Brothers to greenlight it and not fuck it up at this point.
