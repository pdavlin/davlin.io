I’ve been on record for a long time as a Ryan Reynolds hater. I don’t like him as Deadpool, I don’t like Mint Mobile, and I demand sell his stake in the Alpine Formula 1 team. Yet somehow there are parts of this movie that genuinely work. And I still hated it. 

Lots of this movie is creative. It has a garish John Wick sensibility for creative violence. Yes, I got some references. Somehow, Hugh Jackman is still putting in the work for these movies. It’s easily the most entertaining Marvel film in at least five years.

And yet it’s impossible to escape the reality of this movie. It’s frequently visually dull and chock full of plainly awful CGI. The third act is a nightmare to sit through and that’s before the awful decision to hide Jackman’s face behind the worst CGI mask I’ve ever seen.

Like all MCU films, this movie lacks an emotional undercurrent. There’s a real aversion to the kinds of emotional beats that could make these characters interesting. Every emotional buildup is cut down by a bit within seconds. It’s troubling that this is the *Marvel Movie For Adults* and yet it’s completely, profoundly immature. 

Like all superhero films of the 2020s, *Deadpool & Wolverine *invests all its time setting up references to Intellectual Property. It is constantly waiting to reveal a cameo from a 2000s superhero film, introduce a pop song, and make shoutouts to corporations under the guise of multiverse stories. It wastes its cultural capital on this instead of pulling interesting characters from the comics or, god forbid, introducing new ones. 

A review of *The Flash* on Letterboxd compared that film to “rearranging deck chairs on the Titanic.” Superhero films, since then, have only sunk further.
